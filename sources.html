[1] Kazim et al., 2021

"Systematizing audit in algorithmic recruitment", Journal of Intelligence

Researchers from University College London lay out what an audit of a hiring tool should check, including the data, the model, and how it was built. Two of the authors run an AI auditing company, so they have real experience, but also a stake in the auditing business.

[2] Carter, 2024

"Why the algorithmic recruiter discriminates", Maastricht Journal of European and Comparative Law

A Cambridge law researcher explains that AI is so complex and secretive that the evidence needed to prove discrimination usually isn't available. She argues laws should give people more access to that evidence.

[3] Albaroudi et al., 2024

"A comprehensive review of AI techniques for addressing algorithmic bias in job hiring", AI

A review of ways to make hiring AI fairer. It shows bias often starts in the training data, and warns that some fixes may hide bias instead of removing it.

[4] Mihoubi and Salinesi, 2026

"LLMs resistance to discrimination: A study of prompt influence in AI-based resume screening", Springer conference paper

Researchers gave popular AI models 16 equally qualified resumes and changed how the instructions were worded. The wording changed who got picked, and the models covered up their bias with neutral-sounding explanations.

[5] Gaebler et al., 2024

"Auditing large language models for race & gender disparities", Behavioral Science & Policy

Researchers from Harvard, the University of Chicago, and Penn swapped names and pronouns on 801 real teaching job applications and had 11 AI models rate them. They argue this test works better than simply comparing how often different groups get selected, but it still couldn't show where the bias came from.
